The question behind it

Less visiting the list.
More staying in
the conversation.

I kept moving between chats and wondering why returning had to mean navigating back to a list.

Threefold is a way to try another answer. It puts the movement on the conversation itself: pull it aside, glimpse where you are going, or stop and see the whole stack.

It is an experiment to feel on a phone. The distance, shrinking, spring, activity markers and haptics are all adjustable. The point is to find out whether this feels easier.

The sketch above uses real prototype screenshots. Try three-finger touch in Safari on your iPhone; iOS can reserve some gestures for system controls. The native builds add UIKit touch handling and haptics.

Try it on your iPhone.

A Mac with Xcode 26+.
An iPhone with iOS 17+.
Your own Apple Account for signing.

  1. Get the project.

    Download the source ZIP and unzip it, or clone the public repository.

  2. Open it in Xcode.

    Open native/SessionSwipe.xcodeproj. Choose WhatsAppSwipe for the chat demo or SessionSwipe for Codex.

  3. Sign it and run.

    Choose your connected iPhone, select your team in Signing & Capabilities, then press Run. Xcode handles the development signing.

  4. Start with a downward swipe.

    Use three fingers to reveal an older chat. Tap the right-edge indicator whenever you want to open the stack directly.

A few useful details.

Can I try it without a Mac?

Yes: use the browser sketch above to explore the idea. Installing the native builds currently needs Xcode on a Mac. There is no ready-to-install public iPhone package yet.

Does it use my real chats?

No. Both demos contain fictional conversations. Messages you enter, drafts, reading position and settings stay in each app’s local storage. No account or server is involved.

How do I open the stack?

In Safari on your iPhone, use the preview’s 3 fingers option. Place all three fingers inside the phone, drag down, pause briefly while the chat is small, then release when the hint says “Release to browse the stack.” An ordinary swipe switches directly. Browse the open stack with one finger and tap a card. The native apps also have a right-edge indicator to open the stack.

What can I change?

Hold the conversation title or tap the stack’s sliders. Seventeen saved controls let you tune card size, spacing, corners, spring, speed, gesture thresholds, indicators, activity, haptics and fingertip dots.

What about accessibility gestures?

With VoiceOver enabled, the native custom three-finger recognizer is disabled. The visible indicator, menus and accessibility actions still offer navigation. iOS Zoom also uses three-finger gestures. The browser sketch has buttons and keyboard controls: Arrow Down for older, Arrow Up for newer, and Escape to leave the stack.
